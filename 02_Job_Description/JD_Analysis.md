## 2. JD text (condensed, faithful to the original)

**What this role is about.** Redesign construction/design workflows by weaving AI, LLMs and agents into design, drafting and modeling pipelines. Hunt bottlenecks in design software, find repetitive modeling effort, build faster workflows. Be the bridge between architectural/engineering problems and technical AI teams.

**Daily work**
- *Finding and mapping inefficiencies:* shadow design teams; audit CAD/BIM/drafting tools for slow manual tasks; find where data is lost or re-entered between platforms.
- *Designing and launching AI workflows:* redesign processes with prompts, LLMs and agents; test AI construction/design tools with tech teams (safe, secure, working); run small quick pilots before company-wide rollout.
- *Keeping systems sharp:* track drafting speed, modeling accuracy and time savings; tweak rules and prompts to prevent AI mistakes or outdated design outputs; build human-in-the-loop rules so complex engineering decisions go to a person.
- *Bringing people along:* work with legal/compliance on construction regulations, data privacy and IP; coach drafters and modelers through friendly workshops to reduce adoption anxiety.

**Background:** ~5+ years fixing processes (ideally AEC); deep understanding of design, drafting and 3D modeling pipelines; 2+ years putting AI/automation into production; daily use of ChatGPT, Claude or automation platforms.

**Skills:** problem solving (break messy modeling/drafting problems into steps), data smarts (prove where hours are lost), communication (engineers and architects), adaptability.

## 3. What the JD really means (read with the company research)

1. **The experience bar is softer than it looks.** The header says **2–5 years**; the body says "around 5+". About 3 years of experience fits the header band. Quote the header if experience comes up.

2. **It sits in "Admin", not Engineering.** It likely reports to operations/management (e.g., Director of Operations or HR & Back Office), not an AI team. The JD mentions "our technical AI teams", but public research found no AI engineers; production is ~200 people, mostly drafters and trainees. Expect to be the **first AI person** and to build things yourself.

3. **Their business decides what "value" means.**
   - AOP, the flagship service, is priced at **"35% of your typical architectural design budget"** (fixed fee, not hourly). Every drafting/QC hour saved is margin. Frame results as *hours saved per CD set*.
   - AI is also an **existential threat**: they sell drafting hours at under $25/hr while Autodesk claims its AI can handle 80–90% of routine design. They need someone to turn that threat into an advantage.

4. **Known pain points map onto the JD lines.**

| JD line | Company pain behind it |
|---|---|
| "modeling accuracy", "fix errors" | Clutch clients: tasks "marked complete when not finished"; weak local code/zoning knowledge |
| "coach design teams", "adoption anxiety" | Trainee-heavy teams fed by UppAcademy |
| "data lost or re-entered between platforms" | Revit → Bluebeam → Excel → Autodesk Construction Cloud handoffs |
| "human-in-the-loop", "safe, secure" | They never seal drawings (client AOR/EOR approves); client files under NDA; no ISO 27001 |

5. **Their tools:** Revit (primary), AutoCAD, Navisworks, Bluebeam, ACC/BIM 360, Civil 3D, Google Workspace, Dropbox, Zoho (SalesIQ, Campaigns), Keka HR. n8n-style automation fits this stack well.

6. **KPIs they will judge you on:** drafting speed, modeling accuracy rate, time savings, adoption.

## 5. How to position yourself

- **One-line pitch:** "I turn messy production work into measurable workflows: extract and structure the data, automate the deterministic parts, use AI only where judgment is needed, and keep engineers in control."
- Speak their language: CD sets (30/60/100%), AOR/EOR, Second Eye, AOP, Bluebeam markups, ACC issues, schedules, revision clouds.
- Tie every idea to **hours per CD set**, **first-pass QC yield**, and **trainee productivity**.
- First 30 days: shadow QC and production → baseline → pick one narrow pilot → measure → go/no-go.

## 6. Questions to ask them

1. Which workflow consumes the most repetitive human hours today (CD production, QC, revisions, schedules)?
2. Who will this role report to, and is there an existing tech/AI team?
3. What data exists on drafting time, rework and QC findings?
4. What are the client-data rules for external LLM providers (NDA, zero-retention, local models)?
5. What would success look like at 90 days?
6. Have any AI pilots been tried, and why did they stall?

## 7. Do / don't

- **Do:** be transparent about using AI tools (the JD asks for daily ChatGPT/Claude users); report measured numbers; talk about human approval and data security unprompted.
- **Don't:** claim Revit/AutoCAD production experience; promise accuracy you haven't measured; raise negative findings from the company research in the interview.
- **Optional goodwill note:** the website loads a script from `evil.com` on every page. If you mention it, do so briefly and privately to your HR/interview contact, as a helpful heads-up, separate from the task submission.
